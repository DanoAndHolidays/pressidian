# ASCII planet

The hero uses four GPU passes: a mask that isolates the orbiting bodies, a procedural Jupiter system (one gas giant, its equatorial rings and two moons), an ASCII character image, then horizontal tears and edge dispersion. All geometry, materials, glyphs and animation are generated locally; there are no model or texture downloads. A third orbiter — a deep-space station — is built, wired and documented below, but parked behind `STATION_VISIBLE` in `scene.ts`, so the hero currently ships without it.

## Scene and interaction

`scene.ts` renders `main`'s banded gas giant unchanged — sand and cream belts with a blue polar hood, a plain sphere with no oblateness and no storm oval — lit with a ring shadow cast across its disc, behind a single ring spanning 1.32–2.08 with a Cassini gap at 1.78. Added around it are two orbiting moons, plus the parked station. Rings and orbital radii are artistic composition, not a physically scaled simulation.

The two moons are the planet's own orange-red, separated on **hue within that family** rather than on value: Europa is an amber moon at ~27°, its companion a vermilion one at ~18°. That is a correction rather than a preference. A genuinely darker companion was tried first and it disappeared — measured on the rendered hero its disc peaked at scene luma 96 against the backdrop's 27, while the amber one peaked at 195, and the report was exactly that: one moon clear, one invisible. A moon is only a few cells across and spends most of its orbit over the hero's dark backdrop, so luminance is the one axis it cannot spend; hue is free, and it survives the ASCII pass untouched because a body's cell keeps its own colour. The pair is therefore matched in brightness (both peak at 160–195) and told apart by hue, by markings — `uFeature` switches the amber moon's crack lines for the vermilion moon's ringed impact scars — and by the planet, whose ramp sits at 28–34° between them. The two are also within a few percent of each other in radius (0.094 and 0.086), on purpose: a body smaller *and* dimmer than its neighbour loses a third of its ink to the cell grid and reads as a stray glyph cluster instead of a world.

Jupiter's local +Y is the spin axis, so its local XZ plane is the ring plane, and every orbiter is placed on a circle in that plane: `placeOnRingOrbit` positions each body from the two in-plane basis vectors of `jupiter.quaternion` rather than from the world axes. Orbiters stay in `orbitLayer`, a sibling of the `jupiter` group, which keeps their own orientation camera-facing — neither the station's assembly nor a moon's markings may be foreshortened by the planet's tilt. A body's phase therefore changes only its position along the ellipse the rings already draw: it cannot tilt its own orbit, drift above or below the bands, or orbit a point other than the planet's centre.

Radii keep both moons **outside** the ring system and put the station **inside** it, threading the band: the moons orbit at 2.17 and 2.38, each clearing the outer ring edge (2.08) by its own radius, and the station crosses the bands at 1.61. That split is deliberate, and it is what buys the hero its planet size: the camera's half-width is fixed at 2.35 model units, so the planet renders at `scale` x 130 px and the cost of pushing an orbit outward is a smaller share of the frame, not a smaller planet. Moving the station outside as well would push the outermost orbit to 2.53 and spend the margin the moons now use, which is why it stays in the band. The orbits are spaced by more than the sum of the two bodies they separate (0.21 clear against 0.18 of body), so the two moons can never touch, and the outermost projected reach (0.9287 x 2.38 x 0.99 = 2.15) still leaves 26 px of frame at 616 px.

`phase` — the in-plane angle at t = 0 — is a framing decision, not a physical one. The opening frame has the amber moon left of the planet a little above its middle, and the vermilion one **level with that middle on the right**, which is the angle where the projected ellipse crosses mid-height, `-atan(ringPlaneX.y / ringPlaneZ.y)` ≈ -0.68. That puts the vermilion moon on the far half of its orbit, which is where mid-height on the right happens to be, but it projects 1.55 model units from the planet's centre against a limb at 1.0, so nothing occludes it and the opening frame still shows both moons. Its first position was the lower right (phase 0.6, projected at 524 x 375 of 616 x 462), which read as a corner decoration rather than as a body on the ring plane.

The station's own radius is not a free number either, which is why it stays written down next to the moons instead of being re-derived when the flag flips: the assembly is 17 cells across including its strobe booms, and the in-plane reach of that rectangle is 0.47 — a screen-space corner projects into the ring plane at up to 0.93 of its x and 0.54 of its y, because the ring plane is only 22.8° from the screen plane. At 1.61 the station clears the inner moon's own body (2.17 - 0.47 - 0.086) and keeps its inner tip 0.14, about two and a half cells, clear of the planet's limb.

All the bodies cross the planet's silhouette in projection: the closest a body on radius `r` ever comes to the screen centre is 0.39 `r`, inside the planet's radius for every orbit here. That part of each orbit is on the camera side of the planet — world z ≈ 1.3 for the station against the planet's nearest point at 0.99 — so a body transits the disc in front of it rather than vanishing behind.

Every drawn body is raycast-enabled. Drag Jupiter along its projected equator to change yaw around the fixed spin axis. Drag a moon to advance its orbital phase without detaching from its path: the pointer movement is converted from canvas widths into model units, projected onto the orbit's screen-space tangent **where the body currently is**, and the angle advances by that share of the tangent's own length — so the body follows the pointer along its path, one to one on screen, and a moon on a wider orbit turns less for the same pull. A drag straight across the path leaves the body where it is, the way a rail would; a moon cannot be pulled off its orbit or pushed through the planet.

That projection is a correction, and the two things it has to get right are both mistakes that were made here first. `phase += dx` — the unprojected version — read as **inverted** over half of every orbit: the orbit projects to a flat ellipse, screen x runs against the angle along its lower arc, and both moons started on that arc, so dragging right walked the body left. And the tangent has to be taken at the body's whole angle — authored phase plus clock plus drag — not at the drag offset, which begins at zero for every body: at angle zero the path runs vertically, so a projection taken there swallows a horizontal drag whole. `placeOnRingOrbit` therefore records the angle it placed a body at, and the drag re-places the body as it goes, so the scene graph matches a pointer that moves faster than the render clock.

Pointer capture keeps dragging stable outside the original glyph silhouette. Only grab/grabbing cursors indicate interaction; there is no visible instruction or hover label. Touch uses the same constrained gestures. `pick` filters on `visible` before casting, because `Raycaster` does not consult it — a parked station would otherwise keep taking the pointer while drawing nothing.

`postFragment` puts the planet and its rings on `main`'s orange luminance ramp, and nothing else. The orbiters need more than that. Their colour is not merely re-mapped by the ramp: the rings are **transparent**, so they are blended over whatever lies behind them, and a body inside the band arrives already washed to orange before this pass ever sees it — measured on the station at radius 1.61, not one cool pixel survived. So the orbiters get a second colour pass with no rings in it. In that pass the planet draws **depth but no colour**: nothing else would reject a body that has passed behind it, and without it a body on the far side of its orbit painted its own colour over the disc, so the planet looked transparent. Depth-only is the whole trick — it occludes the bodies without adding planet colour to a pass whose job is the bodies' unblended ink. This pass then takes its colour from there wherever a body covers the cell, and from the scene everywhere else. A partially covered cell keeps the planet's strokes around the body instead of being punched out, and the switch between ramp and own colour happens across the middle of the cell's coverage rather than at the first covered pixel, so a body's edge cells do not flicker. Bloom follows whichever ink the cell ended up with, so a cyan beacon glows cyan instead of taking an orange halo. The station is built from unlit materials so its structure reads by ink level rather than by a light, and `moonFragment` writes display-space values like `planetFragment` rather than converting colour spaces on its own.

## Station (parked)

`STATION_VISIBLE` in `scene.ts` is `false`, and that one line is the whole of the parking: the station stays in the scene graph with its orbit, its materials, its animations and its disposal path, and only `visible`, the pointer filter and the render clock consult the flag. Set it to `true` and it returns unchanged; the one thing that then wants a second look is the canvas `aria-label` in `PixelPlanet.vue`, which deliberately names no station while the flag is false.

It is parked because it still does not read as a station at the hero's cell size, and the reason is worth keeping. The assembly is authored **on** the character grid rather than modelled and then sampled by it: every part is a rectangle whose edges fall on cell boundaries — one cell being one character of the 7.5 x 10.5 CSS px grid at the hero's 616 px column, so a design row is 1.4 columns tall — and every part carries an **ink level**, a display luma chosen so the ramp in `postFragment` selects the character that suits it (`floor(clamp(1.15 * level + 0.017, 0, 1) * 10)` is the glyph index). The shape is the standard orbital read: a fifteen-cell truss with four two-cell blankets hung on it, a five-cell core crossing the middle with a module either side, an antenna above and a docked vehicle below. The levels are one glyph band apart on purpose — `'='` blankets, `'+'` truss and vehicle, `'*'` glint peak and core shoulders, `'#'` core, `'%'` dish, `'@'` strobes — because the first version of it used `':'` for the blankets and `'='` for the truss, and measured on the rendered hero those cells carried so little ink that the arrays read as stray dots. At a 7.5 x 10.5 px cell a glyph is a shape first and a density second, so a structure drawn in thin glyphs is a structure nobody can see.

What is still unsolved is sub-cell drift. The station travels about 0.8 cells per second along its orbit, and a body that sits between cells has its ink averaged across two of them — a one-cell truss loses up to half its level and a two-cell blanket dims as a whole, so the assembly breathes between glyph bands as it goes. Snapping the station's screen position to the cell grid would fix that, at the cost of motion that steps once every 1.3 s; the trade was not taken, and it is the first thing to decide if the station comes back.

## Rendering and colour

`scene.ts` owns the renderer, meshes, glyph atlas and three render targets. The scene target stores the shaded system. The orbiter target holds the orbiting bodies alone — same geometry and light, the planet as a depth-only occluder, no rings — at drawing-buffer resolution, so their colour reaches the ASCII pass unblended. The ASCII target is sized to the drawing buffer (DPR capped at 2), with cells measured in CSS pixels (7.5 × 10.5). The final pass samples the finished strokes, so horizontal displacement moves their shapes and alpha together.

The hero column is sized for this scene: `.hero-visual` runs to 780 px in a `.91fr / 1fr` pair of columns, about 100 px wider than before the orbiters existed, which is what keeps the planet's pixel size up as well as its size within the scene.

The palette in `postFragment` is authored in display sRGB to preserve the original artwork. Both intermediate textures explicitly use `NoColorSpace`: the first contains scene data; the second contains already-authored display values that must not be decoded or gamma-encoded again. The final canvas explicitly uses `SRGBColorSpace` and `NoToneMapping`. Do not append `colorspace_fragment` to these display-colour passes without first converting the authored palette to linear values.

The ASCII image and final output store **premultiplied alpha** (`rgb * alpha`), matching the renderer's `premultipliedAlpha: true` context. Linear filtering therefore does not leak bright RGB from transparent pixels into ring/glyph edges. No additional material blending is used for the fullscreen passes. `preserveDrawingBuffer` stays off.

## Glitch

`PLANET_GLITCH` is the share of 8 Hz slots that tear (default 0.21, half the previous 0.42 probability). Each burst still lasts 125 ms. Integer hashing keeps the schedule independent of GPU implementations of `sin()`, while a fresh per-scene seed changes the pattern on each visit. Each burst selects 1–3 regions across 14–82% of the canvas height, with variable horizontal position and length. Regions are 1–3 character rows high and shift left or right by 1–3 cells. Band bounds and cell indices use the same units.

The third pass adds stronger, sharp offset copies 2.4–3.8 CSS pixels to either side of the shifted strokes. The warm accent, vermilion and mixed ink come from the same `--scramble-*` CSS tokens as `DecryptText`, including its OKLab mixtures and theme changes. Torn glyphs also use the title's three ink buckets; undisturbed glyphs keep their original shading. Canvas2D resolves the CSS colours to display-sRGB bytes only at initialization/theme changes, never per frame. Displaced copies retain their full coverage and are composed behind the base stroke with premultiplied alpha, so edges remain visible without bright transparent-pixel leakage.

## Lifecycle

Resize, theme and visibility events only request a frame. Canvas resizing, clearing and all three render passes run together inside `requestAnimationFrame`. Hidden/offscreen scenes pause without rendering; resuming resets the time baseline so background time does not jump the animation. Identical sizes/DPR do not reallocate or clear buffers. Reduced motion draws one static, glitch-free frame after each invalidation and then stops.

A lost WebGL context displays the static ASCII fallback and releases GPU resources. Restoration recreates the scene using the current theme. Unmount disposes both render targets, materials, geometries, atlas and renderer.

## Verification

In development, `window.__planet.force(glitch, time, seed?)` freezes the clock and requests a frame, `release()` resumes and `reset()` restores the preset. These controls are stripped from production. A fixed `time` also pins every orbit, which is how a path is checked against the canvas edge without waiting for a phase to come round. With a fixed time and seed, compare glitch 0 and 1: changes should occupy only a few regions, with visible warm/orange-red edges extending beyond the original strokes. Vary time/seed to inspect position and length diversity. Reduced motion must show the same pixels as glitch 0. With the station parked, a probe placed over the star field where it used to sit must report no grab target — `Raycaster` would otherwise still answer for a body that draws nothing.

A drag is checked the same way, with the clock frozen so the only thing that can move is the body under the pointer. Drag a moon 80 px to the right and it must end up **right of where it started**, about 54 px further along its path at the hero's 616 px column (the gesture projects to 59 px of path, and the chord of that arc is a little shorter), not with its screen x reduced; the two moons start on opposite sides of the planet and both must follow. Drag straight across the path and the body must not move at all. The engine's own `pointerDown`/`pointerMove` can be driven directly, but the real pointer path is `page.mouse`: the bug this replaced was invisible to a state check and only showed up in the rendered frame.

Check light/dark themes, desktop/mobile widths, pointer and touch dragging, DPR 1/2, visibility resume and context loss/restoration. Compare the production build under `/pressidian/` using `scripts/serve-pages.mjs`, not just the Vite development server. A browser-profile-specific brightness difference also requires checking that profile's extensions and GPU settings; the component cannot override third-party page modifications.
